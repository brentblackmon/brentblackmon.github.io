The Analytics Gap in Small and Medium Businesses

Brent Blackmon

Most of the AI conversation is written for companies with data teams, clean pipelines, and a CDO. That's not the world most small and medium businesses live in, and the gap is where the real opportunity is.

Almost everything written about AI adoption assumes a starting point most companies don't have: a modern data warehouse, an analytics team, and executives who already trust the numbers on the dashboard. That's the world of the enterprise. It's not the world of a 250-person business running a decade-old system of record with a couple of people who know how to actually get information out of it. I see this most often in manufacturing and distribution, where I spend most of my time, but the shape of the problem is the same in a services firm, a healthcare group, or a regional retailer.

That gap is the conversation nobody's having, and it's the one that matters for most small and medium businesses.

The data is there. The access isn't.

Small and medium businesses aren't short on data. Whatever system runs the business, an ERP, a practice management platform, a POS system, has been capturing transactions for years. What they're short on is access. In a survey I ran of more than 200 operations leaders in manufacturing and distribution, 35% named business intelligence dashboards as a top priority and 28% said they needed help just standing up analytics. One VP of Operations put it about as plainly as it can be put: "Better reporting fields, easier ability to make custom reports without the need of gurus."

That word, gurus, is the whole problem. In most of these companies, the ability to answer a basic operational question, what's driving margin erosion this month, where inventory is actually sitting, why a customer's order is late, lives with one or two people. Everyone else waits in line. That's not a technology gap. It's an access bottleneck, and it's been there long before anyone started talking about AI.

Readiness and ambition are pulling in opposite directions

Here's the part that should worry more leadership teams than it does: in that same survey, 26% of operations leaders said they're planning to invest in AI, while rating their own organization's readiness at an average of 4 out of 10. Nearly half rated themselves 3 or lower. People want to move. Most don't think they're ready.

That tension is rational. Bolting AI onto data nobody trusts doesn't fix the trust problem, it just makes bad answers arrive faster and with more confidence behind them. The instinct to hesitate isn't wrong. But it usually gets pointed at the wrong fix, which is spending a year cleaning data before doing anything useful with it.

Where the actual opportunity sits

Small and medium businesses have a genuine structural advantage here that almost nobody talks about: the data already exists inside a single system of record. There's no eighteen-month integration project to stitch together six SaaS tools. The core system already has the transactions. The work isn't building a data platform from scratch, it's making what's already there accessible to the people who need it, in language they can use, without waiting on the one person who knows the report writer.

That's a fundamentally smaller, faster problem than the one most AI vendors are selling against. It's also the one worth solving first.
