Your Agents Need a Manager

Brent Blackmon

Part 7, More Agents, Same Constraint, was about adding agents. This one is about managing the ones you already have.

In conversations with business leaders and founders, the same problem keeps coming up: agents nobody can account for. They run every day and burn tokens, but nobody owns them, nobody reviews them, and nobody can say what they earned last quarter. The monitoring tools can now show which agents aren't working. Somebody still has to do something about it.

The staff using these agents usually say they help, and they probably do: emails, meeting notes and other repetitive work take less time. But nobody can trace that time to a specific number.

Would you let one of your direct reports work without a goal or a measure?

That is a management job, and we already know how to do it. We do it for people. At my last company, I worked with a team to harvest our best work into named solutions, and we tracked each one on what it cost to build and how it performed once it was in production. Here is how that works.

Hire it like staff

Name. A name makes it someone's responsibility and something the team talks about.

Job description. What it does, what it is allowed to touch, and what it never does.

Manager. One person owns its output and answers for it in the operating meeting.

One measure, tied to the P&L. Revenue it creates, cost it removes, hours it gives back, or errors it prevents. Set it before go-live. If you can't name the line on the P&L, don't build the agent.

Probation. The first 30 days are a trial with a target. Miss it and the agent never makes the team.

Review it like staff

Once a month the manager sits down with the team that uses the agent and answers four questions:

  1. Did the measure move?
  2. Does the team use it every week without being told to?
  3. How much of its work does a person have to check or redo?
  4. What does each result cost compared with a person doing the same work?

Keep or cut

VerdictWhenWhat happens
Keep and promoteThe measure moved, the team uses it weekly, and it costs less per result than a personMore scope, more data, or a second team
Improvement planPeople use it, but the measure is flat or the rework is high30 days to fix the prompt, the data or the handoff, then the same review
Turn it offNo movement in a quarter, nobody uses it, or checking it costs more than the work it savesShut it down and move the budget to an agent that is producing

Turning an agent off is a good outcome. It frees money and attention for the ones that work, and it keeps everyone honest about what AI is doing for the business.

The question for every operating meeting

What did each agent produce this month, and where does it show up in the numbers?

The AI layer is the amplifier. The operating discipline is still the point.
